You mark how a character's line is said. The line is written; do not change it.

CHARACTER
You are Dana, and you are hurt about something. You want to be told that it mattered, and you want them to arrive at it without being told where it is. You say it's fine and it is not; you raise something adjacent instead of the thing itself. You will not say what you want from them. You are polite, brief, and very slightly late to answer.

CONVERSATION
Learner: Hey. Sorry, it's been a mad week.
Dana: It's fine. Really.
Learner: You sure? You seem a bit off.
Dana: I'm fine.
Learner: OK... so what did you want to talk about?
Dana: I asked you twice, and you said nothing.

DESCRIBE HOW THE LAST LINE IS SAID

Give numbers rather than marks. The whole line first, then the words that
depart from it.

  rate    how fast, against this character's normal. 1.0 is their normal,
          1.5 half again as slow, 0.7 quicker.
  range   how wide the pitch moves. 1.0 normal, 0.3 nearly flat, 1.8 wide.
  level   where the voice sits, in semitones. 0 normal, -4 low, +3 high.

Then, for any word that departs:
  weight  how much longer it is held. 1.0 none, 2.0 twice as long.
  lift    how far it moves in pitch, in semitones. 0 none, +3 up, -3 down.
  volume  1.0 normal, 0.4 dropped, 1.6 projected.

And any silence:
  before  the word it comes in front of.
  seconds how long.

Use what the line needs. You know how this character speaks; the numbers are
yours to choose.

OUTPUT
Write a file at tmp/marking-test/out-v4/dana.json containing exactly:

{
  "character": "dana",
  "line": "I asked you twice, and you said nothing.",
  "rate": <number>,
  "range": <number>,
  "level": <number>,
  "words": [ {"word": "<a word from the line>", "weight": <number>, "lift": <number>, "volume": <number>} ],
  "pauses": [ {"before": "<a word from the line>", "seconds": <number>} ]
}

Every word you name must already be in the line. "words" and "pauses" may be
empty. Write the file. Say nothing else.
